# Fairness-aware CP experiments hinge on cell sizes

Fairness-aware conformal prediction (CP) papers and semi-synthetic bias-injection studies have converged on a recognisable template, but the two literatures have never been joined on credit data, and the places where they are weakest are exactly the places a credit-scoring thesis is most exposed. The CP papers (Romano et al. 2020, Zhou & Sesia 2024, Cresswell et al. 2025, Lu et al. 2022) almost always use α = 0.1, a single hidden sensitive attribute, a 50/50 train–calibration split of a modest pool, a fixed test set and 40–200 repetitions, and they report means with at most 2 SE error bars; none sizes the test set from the smallest group, and only Zhou & Sesia and Cresswell et al. run any group-level significance test. The bias-injection literature (Ceccon et al. 2025, Wang et al. 2021, Legast et al. 2026, Bias on Demand, Pombal et al. 2022) agrees that **group-dependent label bias is the one mechanism that reliably produces monotone dose–response curves**, that under-representation mostly moves the *variance* of fairness rather than its mean, that bias belongs in training (and, for CP, calibration) but never in the test set, and that strength grids should start well below 0.1 because tiny one-sided noise already overturns audit conclusions. The decisive constraint for the thesis is arithmetic: every guarantee in this literature is a per-cell guarantee, and the AFCP code's silent default of label-conditional calibration (`conditional=True`) turns every (group × label) cell into the unit of analysis, so a 1,000-row South German Credit split has roughly 25 young defaulters per cell (standard error ≈ 6 pp at 90 % coverage), Give Me Some Credit's under-25 defaulters are 0.15 % of the data, and only the Taiwan data can resolve 3–5 pp group gaps within a single split. The recommendations at the end therefore prioritise cell-size-driven split design, early-stopped and degeneracy-checked classifiers, explicit reporting of the calibration regime, Ceccon-style one-sided label flipping injected into train + calibration with a grid that includes 0.05, Cresswell's set-size and singleton criteria alongside coverage, paired-by-seed tests, and a checklist of AFCP implementation pitfalls (10 % t-test level, label intersection, saturated scores, a selection-frequency bug) that the thesis must document rather than inherit.

## Fairness-aware CP papers share one template and one blind spot

The design cards of the key papers line up more neatly than their framings suggest. Romano, Barber, Sabatti and Candès introduced "equalized coverage" on MEPS regression data (n = 15,656, race as the attribute, 80/20 train/test with calibration carved from the training half, 40 random splits, α = 0.1) and computed the (1−α)(1+1/n) quantile separately within each group; their headline numbers were that marginal CP covered non-white at 0.920 and white at 0.871 while the conditional version hit 0.904/0.902 at the cost of wider white-group intervals (3.150 vs 2.738), and they reported no standard errors at all ([Romano et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05428)). Zhou and Sesia's AFCP, the method the thesis centres on, uses the same skeleton at much smaller scale: total samples of **200–2,000 (synthetic) or 200–5,000 (Nursery), split 50/50 into training and calibration, a fixed 500-point test set, and 100 independent experiments with 2 SE error bars**, with Blue (the hard group) at 10 % of the data so that n = 200 leaves roughly ten Blue calibration points ([Zhou & Sesia 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15106)). Cresswell et al. used single fixed splits (FACET 1,400/4,000/1,400; BiosBias 5,000/10,000/2,000; RAVDESS 240/840/360), RAPS or SAPS scores with Optuna-tuned hyperparameters, and Mondrian-by-group calibration as the "conditional" arm, and were explicit that their Older group with only 91 test examples weakened the group-level conclusions ([Cresswell et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01888)). Lu et al. applied group-conditional APS/RAPS (GAPS/GRAPS) to Fitzpatrick17k skin-type groups with five initialisations, no split sizes and no variability measures ([Lu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04392)). Gibbs, Cherian and Candès ran 200 random 650/650/694 splits on Communities and Crime and derived that the unrandomised group-coverage upper bound is 1−α + |G|/((n+1)P(X∈G)), i.e. the conservativeness of group-conditional sets scales inversely with group frequency ([Gibbs et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12616)).

The shared blind spot is that **no paper derives the test-set or calibration-set size from the smallest group**; the closest the literature comes is Cresswell's remark about 91 test points and the 2026 virtual-screening paper's admission that "roughly 28 compounds per split" in ClinTox's minority class made coverage estimates coarse and explained a 94.6 % overshoot ([Tursunbadalov & Tursunbadalov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06605)). The AFCP authors themselves concede the consequences: at small n the selection test "often selects no attribute", AFCP shows slight Blue under-coverage, and the method "does not always identify the most relevant sensitive attribute" at limited sample sizes ([Zhou & Sesia 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15106)). The thesis's own result files (n_data from 1,000 to 16,000 with delta1 = 0.9, conditional = True, seed 2023) sit squarely in the regime where these caveats bite, and the sample-size sweep is the right instrument for showing when they stop biting.

## Label-conditional calibration is AFCP's silent default, and rare classes pay for it

The single most consequential detail in the AFCP code is invisible in the paper's main text. The paper presents marginal, Exhaustive, Partial and AFCP sets as marginal procedures (Eq. 1, 2, 9) and relegates the label-conditional variant to Appendix A3, yet in `methods.py` **every method class (`Marginal_Selection`, `Partial_Selection`, `Exhaustive_Selection`) has `conditional=True` as its default**, implemented as `idx_y = np.where(Y_calib == y) if conditional else np.arange(len(Y_calib))`, and both experiment scripts carry `conditional_ = True` as the commented default ([AFCP repository](https://github.com/FionaZ3696/Adaptively-Fair-Conformal-Prediction)). Any thesis run that did not flip this flag is a label-conditional experiment, and the Project's result filenames (`conditionalTrue`) confirm that is the case.

Why this matters for binary credit data is well documented in the class-conditional literature. Ding et al. show that a class with fewer than 1/α − 1 = 9 calibration points gets an infinite quantile, so every prediction set contains that label regardless of the scores; that per-class coverage is Beta-distributed with variance ≈ α(1−α)/(|I_y|+2), so ten calibration points at a 90 % target give P(coverage < 80 %) ≈ 0.107; and that the usable regime starts at roughly 20–40 examples per class for the softmax score and 50–100 for RAPS ([Ding et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09335)). The flip side is that marginal calibration is not a safe alternative when a class is rare: in a 2026 benchmark on 15 binary OpenML tabular datasets (including `credit_g` and `uci_default`) across seven classifiers and ten seeds, **average minority-class coverage under marginal CP was 30.5 %**, 57.7 % on `uci_default`, while Mondrian-by-class calibration raised it to 92.2 % at the price of mean set size 1.04 → 1.36 and a non-singleton rate of 12.5 % → 38.3 % ([Singh et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27143v1)). The molecular binary benchmark gives the dose–response: marginal minority coverage of 89.4 / 64.8 / 38.9 / 4.2 % at imbalance ratios 1.19 / 3.26 / 5.18 / 12.2, restored to ≈90 % by Mondrian, with set-size cost "concentrated on the hard class, growing with imbalance", and the mechanism is a conservation identity, minority shortfall = majority surplus × π_maj/π_min, which temperature scaling cannot fix because the pooled quantile, not confidence, is the problem ([Tursunbadalov & Tursunbadalov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06605)). Vadlamani et al. make the bound explicit when group and label are crossed: coverage on the (g, ỹ) cell lies in [1−α, 1−α + 1/(|D_calib(g,ỹ)|+1)] ([Vadlamani et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16115)).

The implication for the thesis is that marginal versus label-conditional is not a nuisance flag but a primary experimental factor. Give Me Some Credit has a 6.7 % default rate ([Lessmann et al. 2015](https://crc.business-school.ed.ac.uk/sites/crc/files/2023-10/Benchmarking-State-of-the-Art-Classification-Algorithms-for-Credit-Scoring-Lessmann-Seow-Baesens-and-Thomas.pdf)), so a marginal procedure will produce near-singleton sets that systematically miss defaulters, while a label-conditional procedure with a selected group (AFCP under `conditional=True`) partitions the calibration set into (group × label) cells whose smallest member is what the Ding/Vadlamani bounds refer to. Both regimes should be run and reported side by side, with per-class and per-(group, class) coverage, because reporting only marginal coverage and mean set size hides the entire phenomenon.

## Base classifiers are treated as black boxes, but over-trained MLPs poison scores and selection tests

CP papers rarely prescribe a training protocol, and where they do the choices diverge sharply. Romano et al. regularised carefully (2×64 ReLU nets, Adam, lr 5e-4, weight decay 1e-6, dropout 0.1, up to 1,000 epochs with early stopping chosen by cross-validation) ([Romano et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05428)); Lu et al. used early stopping with patience 20, MC dropout and Platt scaling ([Lu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04392)); the RAPS authors temperature-scaled every classifier before CP ([Angelopoulos et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193)). AFCP, by contrast, describes a five-layer ReLU network trained with Adam at lr 1e-4 whose loss "demonstrate[s] convergence after 100 epochs" ([Zhou & Sesia 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15106)), but the code tells a different story: `ClassNNet` is 256-256-128-64-K with `use_dropout=False`, the commented default is **`n_epoch = 500`, `Blackbox.full_train` runs to `max_epoch` and saves the final weights with no early stopping or best-epoch checkpoint**, and the "validation" loader is the calibration set itself, used only to log loss ([AFCP repository](https://github.com/FionaZ3696/Adaptively-Fair-Conformal-Prediction)). On a few hundred training rows of South German Credit, that network reaches near-zero training loss and saturated outputs.

Saturation matters more than mis-calibration per se. The coverage guarantee survives any model, but the Gentle Introduction notes that softmax outputs can be "arbitrarily overfit or otherwise untrustworthy" and that exact coverage needs continuous scores or random tie-breaking, a randomisation "usually ignored in practice" ([Angelopoulos & Bates 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511)); the RAPS paper requires label-ordering ties to be broken randomly ([Angelopoulos et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193)); Xi et al. warn that extreme sharpening truncates tail probabilities to exactly zero and produces "meaningless prediction sets" ([Xi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04344v3)); and Dabah and Tirer show that softening an over-confident network barely moves the 1−p̂ score's sets but inflates APS sets (5.31 → 9.14 on CIFAR-100), so temperature should be a separate CP tuning knob rather than a probability-calibration afterthought ([Dabah & Tirer 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05806)). The mechanism that is specific to AFCP is a reasoned inference rather than a published finding, and the thesis should label it as such: the selection test is a one-sample t-test on the Bernoulli leave-one-out miscoverage indicators of the worst group (`stats.ttest_1samp(miscov_ind, alpha + ttest_delta, alternative='greater')`), so if an over-trained network outputs p̂(y_true) ≈ 0 for an entire subgroup, every indicator in that cell is identical, the test statistic is undefined, and the selection step degenerates silently, while if the saturated share of a class exceeds α the class quantile hits the maximum score and every set contains that label ([AFCP repository](https://github.com/FionaZ3696/Adaptively-Fair-Conformal-Prediction); [Ding et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09335)).

Two further facts argue for a weaker baseline next to the MLP. The 2026 tabular benchmark used no neural network at all (HistGradientBoosting, L2 logistic regression, random forest, extra trees, GBM, AdaBoost, naive Bayes) and found the under-coverage/Mondrian pattern qualitatively identical across all seven families ([Singh et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27143v1)); the molecular study reproduced the same minority shortfall with a random forest (64.8 %), a GCN (63.3 %) and ChemBERTa + logistic regression (72.4 %), attributing it to "baseline minority calibration, not architecture" ([Tursunbadalov & Tursunbadalov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06605)). Krstajic's binary-CP caution is the degeneracy check to adopt verbatim: a model with calibration-set AUROC 0.527 produced perfectly valid but useless all-"both" sets, so the base model's AUROC on the calibration set must be reported next to every CP metric ([Krstajic 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10034)). Credit-scoring sanity ranges are well established: German Credit AUC ≈ 0.78–0.81 (LR 0.784, RF 0.789), GMSC ≈ 0.86 for tree ensembles but only 0.693 for raw-feature logistic regression, Taiwan ≈ 0.73–0.77 ([Lessmann et al. 2015](https://crc.business-school.ed.ac.uk/sites/crc/files/2023-10/Benchmarking-State-of-the-Art-Classification-Algorithms-for-Credit-Scoring-Lessmann-Seow-Baesens-and-Thomas.pdf); [Kozodoi et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01907v3); [Hurlin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.10200)).

## Group-wise coverage gaps need hundreds of points per cell

Published practice gives two anchors for sizing. The Gentle Introduction's Table 1 says 1,000 calibration points typically yield coverage between 0.88 and 0.92, that n = 102 is needed for ±5 pp with probability 0.9 and 2,491 for ±1 pp, and that evaluations "require running the entire conformal procedure on different splits of data at least 100 times" ([Angelopoulos & Bates 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511)); Ding et al. supply the per-cell Beta variance quoted above ([Ding et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09335)). Everything else is standard binomial arithmetic, which the research notes derived rather than quoted and which the thesis can present the same way. Within a cell of m test points at true coverage 0.9, the standard error is √(0.09/m): **4.2 pp at m = 50, 3.0 at 100, 2.1 at 200, 1.3 at 500**. Detecting a two-group difference with two-sided α = 0.05 and 80 % power requires approximately 7.85·[p₁(1−p₁) + p₂(1−p₂)]/Δ² points per group per split: **≈1,770 per group for a 3 pp gap, ≈680 for 5 pp, ≈200 for 10 pp**. Averaging over R splits of the same finite pool shrinks the test-sampling component by less than 1/√R and leaves the calibration component α(1−α)/(n_cal+2) untouched, which is why the AFCP curves look smooth only because they average 100 draws of 500 fresh synthetic test points.

Applied to the three datasets with the conventional group definitions, the arithmetic is stark. South German Credit has 1,000 rows, 300 bad, and the under-25 group is 19 % with a 42.1 % bad rate ([Le Quy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00530); [UCI 522](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/522/south+german+credit)); an AFCP-style design with 300 test and 350/350 train/calibration leaves roughly 57 young test points (SE ≈ 4 pp) and about 24 young defaulters in test and 28 in calibration (SE ≈ 6 pp; label-conditional quantile index 27 of 28), so only ≥10 pp group gaps are visible per split and anything finer needs the per-split gap paired across ≥100 splits. Give Me Some Credit has 150,000 rows at 6.7 % default, and the under-25 group used by Kozodoi et al. is 2 % of applicants ([Kozodoi et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01907v3)); counts computed from the Project's raw `cs-training.csv` put age < 25 at 1.38 % with a 10.5 % default rate, so the (young × default) cell is ≈0.15 % of rows, i.e. about 14 points in a 10,000-row calibration set (SE ≈ 8 pp) and 72 in a 50,000-row one (SE ≈ 3.5 pp). GMSC's demographic alternative, age ≥ 60 (32 % of rows, 3.1 % default by the same computation), gives a 1 % cell but flips the direction of disadvantage, and no fairness paper standardises an elderly cut. Taiwan is the only comfortable case: 30,000 rows, 22.1 % default, sex 39.6 % male with a 24.2 % male default rate ([Le Quy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00530)), so a (male × default) cell is ≈9.6 % of rows, ≈960 points in a 10,000-row test set (SE ≈ 1 pp), and 3–5 pp gaps are resolvable in one split.

|---|---|---|---|
| SGC (age ≤ 25, bad) | ≈8 % | 40 / – / – | ≥10 pp, aggregated over ≥100 splits |

Repetition and testing practice is clearer than sizing practice. AFCP and the Gentle Introduction use 100 splits with 2 SE bars; the two 2026 tabular benchmarks use ten seeds with mean ± SD, percentile-bootstrap CIs over seeds (1,000–10,000 resamples), paired two-tailed or one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (whose p-value floor for ten pairs is 9.8e-4) and a Friedman–Nemenyi critical-difference diagram; the molecular paper additionally reports the seed-to-seed SD (±7.0 pp marginal, ±3.0 pp Mondrian) and the per-split range (38–80 %) of minority coverage ([Singh et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27143v1); [Tursunbadalov & Tursunbadalov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06605)). The ssbc guide adds Clopper–Pearson intervals for singleton and abstention rates and a PAC correction that tightens α to ≈0.057 for n = 50 at δ = 0.05 ([ssbc guide](https://ssbc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html)). The 2026 benchmarks' practice of pairing by seed is the right primitive for method contrasts; comparing *groups* within a method requires either pooled binomial CIs or a paired-by-seed test on the per-split gap.

## Label bias moves the needle; subsampling mostly moves the variance

The bias-injection literature converges on four mechanisms (group-dependent label flipping or score-shifting, group-dependent undersampling, proxy or feature perturbation, and prevalence or separability disparities), almost always applied to a single designated disadvantaged group with a single scalar strength ([Baumann et al. 2023](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/29507/2/2023_Baumann-etal_Bias-on-demand_ACM.pdf); [Ceccon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08866)). The effectiveness ranking is consistent across independent studies. Ceccon et al., whose seven datasets include German Credit with age as the attribute, found label bias "has a large effect" (Adult EO gap 0.08 → 0.55 as the flip fraction f goes 0 → 1) while under-representation "has a small effect on EO" and is, in their words, "overemphasized"; crucially, the label-bias effect is conditional on how strongly non-sensitive features proxy the attribute (sAUC > 0.9 datasets react, COMPAS and Folktables "show little significant change even at f = 1") ([Ceccon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08866)). Legast et al. reached the same verdict ("Label bias is very harmful. Selection bias is often negligible when ... features are predictive, but it matters for small datasets or harder tasks") with a score-shift mechanism, S_b = S − β_l·A·scale + N, that concentrates flips near the decision boundary ([Legast et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09662v1)). Pombal et al. found that group-size disparity leaves models "fair in expectation" but produces high fairness *variance* so some seeds are unfair, and that prevalence disparity shifts FPR/FNR in predictable directions ([Pombal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.06273)); Zhioua et al. showed small or imbalanced samples usually *under*-estimate discrimination and that AUC is far less sensitive than FPR/EO ([Zhioua et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17956v1)). Fogliato et al. supply the lower end of the grid: on COMPAS, just 20–30 hidden recidivists (α ≈ 0.03–0.07) break calibration tests, so a dose–response grid that starts at 0.1 misses the regime where audit conclusions already flip ([Fogliato et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.13808)).

Where to inject is nearly unanimous. Ceccon et al. use an 80-10-10 split with bias "injected only into train and validation. The test set is kept unbiased" ([Ceccon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08866)); Wang et al. add noise only to training and measure on a clean 20 % test set ([Wang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00379)); Bias on Demand trains on the proxy label P_Y and scores against the true Y, precisely to show that criteria conditioning on Y "can make a model look fair and accurate against P_Y while it is not against Y" ([Baumann et al. 2023](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/29507/2/2023_Baumann-etal_Bias-on-demand_ACM.pdf)). Biasing the test set is justified only when the object of study is the audit itself (Fogliato; Legast's finding that biased test sets manufacture an apparent fairness–accuracy trade-off). For conformal prediction the natural analogue of "train-only" is Einbinder et al.'s protocol, which corrupts **training and calibration labels and keeps test labels clean**, and whose results are directly relevant: with a 5 % flip rate, uniform noise makes sets *conservative* (≈94 % against a 90 % target) while biased "wrong-to-right" or rare-to-frequent noise *under*-covers (≈85–87 %), and APS was more robust than the hinge score ([Einbinder et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14295)). No source injects group-dependent label bias and then measures group-conditional conformal coverage, and none does so on Taiwan, GMSC or Home Credit; German Credit appears only as one of many benchmarks (Ceccon with age; Wang with gender and the rates female ε⁻/ε⁺ = 0.45/0.15, male 0.35/0.55) ([Wang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00379)). That gap is the thesis's contribution and should be stated as such.

Grids are coarse and ad hoc: Ceccon's f, u ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.8, 1} (with 1.0 replaced by 0.95 for SD computations), Wang's symmetric ε ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4} on one group, Legast's ten levels in [0, 1], Bias on Demand's unitless 0–9 axis, Pombal's group-size {0.5, 0.9, 0.99} and prevalence ratio {2, 4} ([Ceccon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08866); [Wang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00379); [Legast et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09662v1); [Pombal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.06273)). Repetitions are 5–10 seeds for label bias (Ceccon 10, Wang 5, Legast 5–10 folds reusing the same partition across bias levels) and 30–50 for sampling-bias studies (Zhioua 30, Chen et al. 50), reflecting the finding that representation manipulations change variance rather than mean ([Zhioua et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17956v1); [Chen et al. 2018](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1805.12002)). Presentation is dose–response line plots of gap versus strength with mean ± SD, or, in the lending-adjacent fraud work, log₂ ratios of group error rates against 0.8/1.25 "80 % rule" bands ([Jesus et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13358.pdf)). AFCP's own two mechanisms deserve scrutiny before reuse on binary data: the synthetic `data_model` makes a fraction delta_g of group g draw its label uniformly from one *half* of the K classes, and the Nursery script adds Uniform(−4, 4) noise to the label and clips to the label range after down-sampling the biased group to 500 rows ([AFCP repository](https://github.com/FionaZ3696/Adaptively-Fair-Conformal-Prediction)). With K = 2, "one half of the classes" is a single class chosen by X₁, which is the same rule as the clean label, so the synthetic noise mechanism collapses toward a no-op in the binary case, and the Nursery mechanism becomes near-complete randomisation; both should be verified in code before any binary adaptation is reported as "the AFCP noise model".

## Credit datasets carry conventions the thesis must adopt or explicitly depart from

The dominant convention is one binary attribute per dataset. For German Credit, AIF360's `GermanDataset` defaults to sex and age with privileged `age > 25`, attributed to Kamiran and Calders (2009) ([AIF360 docs](https://aif360.readthedocs.io/en/latest/modules/generated/aif360.datasets.GermanDataset.html)); Kozodoi et al. use age < 25 as the only sensitive attribute across seven credit datasets (German 19 %, Taiwan 14 %, GMSC 2 % protected) because Kamiran and Calders "found the greatest disparate impact at 25 years" ([Kozodoi et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01907v3)); Le Quy et al. report the group statistics (German: 690 male / 310 female, bad rate 35.2 % vs 27.7 %; age 810 / 190, bad rate 42.1 % young vs 27.2 %; Taiwan: 11,888 male / 18,112 female, default 24.2 % vs 20.8 %) ([Le Quy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00530)). Note that papers disagree on which side 25 falls (AIF360 ≤ 25 unprivileged; Kozodoi < 25), and that a credit-regulation-aware paper excludes gender entirely because its use "is explicitly prohibited" while keeping the 25 cut "for reasons of comparability" even though weight-of-evidence suggested 23 ([arXiv 2209.07912](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.07912)).

Data quality is where a thesis most easily claims a "standard" that does not exist. The Statlog German Credit code table is wrong for attributes A1, A3, A4, A6, A9, A12, A15 and A20, and Grömping's South German Credit (CC BY 4.0) corrects it, but even there "sex cannot be fully recovered" because level 2 of `famges` merges female non-singles with male singles ([UCI 522](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/522/south+german+credit)); the response coding differs between versions (SGC 0 = bad, 1 = good; Statlog 1 = good, 2 = bad). GMSC's only demographic column is age, and counts computed from the Project's raw file show 269 rows carrying sentinel 96/98 in all three delinquency counters simultaneously (54.6 % default among them), one age = 0, 19.8 % missing MonthlyIncome, utilisation ratios up to 50,708 and 609 duplicate rows; Lessmann et al. mean/mode-impute, Kozodoi et al. do not describe their handling at all, and no fairness paper documents its treatment of 96/98 ([Lessmann et al. 2015](https://crc.business-school.ed.ac.uk/sites/crc/files/2023-10/Benchmarking-State-of-the-Art-Classification-Algorithms-for-Credit-Scoring-Lessmann-Seow-Baesens-and-Thomas.pdf); [Kozodoi et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01907v3); [OpenML 45577](https://www.openml.org/api/v1/json/data/45577)). Taiwan's UCI page documents SEX, EDUCATION 1–4, MARRIAGE 1–3 and PAY codes −1 and 1–9 but not EDUCATION 0/5/6, MARRIAGE 0 or PAY −2/0 ([UCI 350](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/350/default+of+credit+card+clients)); the common recode folds EDUCATION 0/5/6 into 4 and MARRIAGE 0 into 3, yielding Le Quy's 377 "others" ([Medium walkthrough](https://medium.com/@agrawal.sourabh644/default-of-credit-card-clients-dataset-a31ec2ffdea4); [Le Quy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00530)), and Kozodoi (23,531 rows) and Hurlin (25,983 rows) each drop rows without saying which ([Hurlin et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.10200)). Fairness papers do not resample for class imbalance; they report AUC or balanced accuracy and leave priors alone ([arXiv 2412.20298](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20298v1); [arXiv 2209.07912](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.07912)). Both UCI datasets are CC BY 4.0; GMSC's licence is unverified beyond OpenML's "Public" tag, so it should be committed as a download script plus hash rather than as data.

## Prioritised design recommendations for the thesis

The recommendations are ordered by how much they change the validity of the comparison between Marginal CP, Partial/Exhaustive equalized coverage and AFCP; the first four are prerequisites, the rest are reporting and extension choices.

**1. Size every split from the smallest (group × label) cell, not from the dataset (highest priority).** Fix a rule such as "≥50 calibration points and ≥100 test points in the smallest cell for a label-conditional run" and derive n_cal, n_test per dataset from it, citing Ding et al.'s 1/α − 1 degeneracy threshold and 20–40-per-class usable regime ([Ding et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09335)), Vadlamani's 1/(|D_calib(g,ỹ)|+1) over-coverage bound ([Vadlamani et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16115)), and the Gentle Introduction's Table 1 ([Angelopoulos & Bates 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511)). Concretely: South German Credit cannot satisfy the rule, so present it as the small-n regime (300 test, 350/350 train/calibration, ≥100 random splits, per-split gaps paired across splits, and an honest statement that only ≥10 pp group gaps are resolvable); Taiwan should use ≈10,000 test with calibration swept over {500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 10,000} to reproduce AFCP's sample-size curve on real data; GMSC should use ≥30,000 test and a calibration sweep up to ≥50,000, with the under-25 group retained for comparability with Kozodoi et al. ([Kozodoi et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.01907v3)) but with the ≈0.15 % cell share stated up front, and an age ≥ 60 sensitivity analysis flagged as non-standard. Report the per-cell count table in the thesis.

**2. Make marginal versus label-conditional calibration an explicit factor and name the AFCP default.** Run every method under both `conditional=False` and `conditional=True`, state that the AFCP code defaults to label-conditional although the paper's main text presents marginal sets ([AFCP repository](https://github.com/FionaZ3696/Adaptively-Fair-Conformal-Prediction); [Zhou & Sesia 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15106)), and report per-class and per-(group × class) coverage, mean set size and {0,1}-set rate for each. Expect marginal to under-cover defaulters on GMSC in line with the 30.5 % average minority coverage of the tabular benchmark and the 4.2 %-at-12:1 result ([Singh et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27143v1); [Tursunbadalov & Tursunbadalov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06605)), and label-conditional to inflate sets on the rare class; the thesis's contribution is quantifying that trade-off per group on credit data.

**3. Early-stop the MLP, add a weak baseline, and run degeneracy checks before any CP step.** Replace AFCP's fixed-epoch training (code default 500 epochs, no dropout, no checkpoint) with a held-out validation slice carved from the *training* half, early stopping on validation log-loss with patience ≈20 as in Lu et al. ([Lu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04392)) or cross-validated stopping as in Romano et al. ([Romano et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05428)), and keep dropout/weight decay on; never use the calibration set for model selection. Run L2 logistic regression and a shallow gradient-boosting model (≤100 trees, depth 3) through the identical CP pipeline, as the tabular benchmark does ([Singh et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27143v1)), because the imbalance findings are architecture-independent ([Tursunbadalov & Tursunbadalov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06605)). Before calibration, report calibration-set AUROC and log-loss (Krstajic's 0.527 example is the cautionary tale) ([Krstajic 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10034)), the fraction of calibration points with p̂(y_true) below 1e-6 and the fraction of tied scores by group, and check AUC against the published ranges (German ≈0.78–0.81, GMSC ≈0.86 non-linear / 0.69 raw LR, Taiwan ≈0.75–0.77) ([Lessmann et al. 2015](https://crc.business-school.ed.ac.uk/sites/crc/files/2023-10/Benchmarking-State-of-the-Art-Classification-Algorithms-for-Credit-Scoring-Lessmann-Seow-Baesens-and-Thomas.pdf)). Keep randomised APS scores (AFCP's default) and state that randomisation is on, since non-randomised APS is conservative and ties break the exact guarantee ([Angelopoulos et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193)).

**4. Audit and document the AFCP implementation pitfalls instead of inheriting them.** (a) The selection t-test runs at `sig_level = 0.1` in code while the paper says "any desired level, like 5 %"; report the level used and run a sensitivity over {0.05, 0.10} and `ttest_delta` ∈ {0, 0.02, 0.05}, plus the AFCP1 arm (test skipped, worst attribute always selected) as the selection-power ceiling ([AFCP repository](https://github.com/FionaZ3696/Adaptively-Fair-Conformal-Prediction); [Zhou & Sesia 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15106)). (b) An attribute is selected only if flagged for *every* placeholder label (Eq. 7); in binary credit data that means the rare-class cell, with few LOO indicators, vetoes selection, so report per-label flag frequencies and consider an "any-label" ablation labelled as a deviation. (c) The test is a one-sample t-test on Bernoulli LOO indicators of the worst group; saturated scores make the indicator vector constant and the statistic undefined, so log the indicator variance per cell and treat NaN statistics as a reportable failure mode rather than "no selection". (d) The final AFCP set is the *union* of the selected-group set and the plain set, which bounds it below by marginal/label-conditional set size. (e) `mc_real.py` computes `selected_3` and `selected_6` from `x == [0]`, so selection-frequency columns for attributes 3 and 6 are unreliable if that script was adapted. (f) `beta = 0` disables the minimum-group-frequency filter; say so. (g) Verify, by inspection of `data_gen.py`, that the "impossible-to-classify" noise mechanism does what it claims when K = 2 before describing any binary synthetic arm as the AFCP noise model.

**5. Adopt Ceccon-style one-sided label flipping as the primary injection, into train + calibration, with a grid that starts at 0.05.** Flip a fraction f of the disadvantaged group's *good* (non-default) training and calibration labels to *bad*, the lending analogue of Bias on Demand's measurement bias on Y and of Ceccon's label bias, with f ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40}, which sits inside Wang's ≤0.4 envelope and Ceccon's one-sided range while adding the low-dose points Fogliato shows already overturn audits ([Ceccon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08866); [Wang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00379); [Fogliato et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.13808); [Baumann et al. 2023](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/29507/2/2023_Baumann-etal_Bias-on-demand_ACM.pdf)). Keep the test set clean in every arm, and run two injection scopes: train-only (does CP repair a biased model?) and train + calibration following Einbinder et al. (does the guarantee itself survive, measured against clean labels?), noting Einbinder's prediction that one-sided noise is the case that under-covers while symmetric noise only inflates sets ([Einbinder et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14295)). Add a score-shift variant (Legast's β_l on the logit) only if time permits, and a representation-bias arm (retain r ∈ {1, 0.5, 0.2, 0.1} of the disadvantaged group) framed as a variance experiment with ≥30 seeds ([Legast et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09662v1); [Pombal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.06273); [Zhioua et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17956v1)). Reuse the same partition across bias levels within a seed, as Legast et al. do, to keep dose–response curves clean, and expect the label-bias effect to be weaker on datasets whose non-sensitive features proxy the attribute poorly ([Ceccon et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08866)). Run Taiwan first: it has the attributes (sex, marriage, education, age) that let AFCP's selection step do real work and the cell sizes to detect its effect.

**6. Report the union of the literature's metrics, per group and per class, and test method contrasts paired by seed.** Per split: marginal coverage, per-class coverage, per-(group × class) coverage, mean set size, singleton rate, {0,1}-set rate, empty-set rate, accuracy given a singleton (selective accuracy), and for AFCP the selection frequency of each attribute and per-label flag frequency ([Krstajic 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10034); [ssbc guide](https://ssbc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html); [Zhou & Sesia 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15106)). Summaries: Δ_Cov (max pairwise group coverage gap) and Δ_Size (max pairwise group set-size gap) from Cresswell et al., together with the group gap in singleton frequency, since Cresswell's human-subject results found Equalized Coverage sets produced the *most* disparate downstream accuracy and that set-size and singleton gaps, not coverage gaps, tracked disparate impact ([Cresswell et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01888)); Ding's CovGap and Lu's pairwise coverage/set-size disparities as secondary ([Ding et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09335); [Lu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04392)); and, for the credit-native presentation, log₂ ratios of group error rates against 0.8/1.25 bands ([Jesus et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13358.pdf)). Statistics: mean ± SD and 2 SE over ≥100 splits for the CP comparison and ≥10 seeds per bias level, percentile-bootstrap CIs over seeds, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank for method-versus-method contrasts (p-floor 9.8e-4 at ten pairs), Clopper–Pearson intervals for per-split singleton and {0,1} rates, and min/max worst-group coverage across seeds, which the 2026 benchmarks report and the fairness-CP papers omit ([Singh et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27143v1); [Tursunbadalov & Tursunbadalov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06605)). For group-versus-group within a method, test the per-split gap paired across seeds rather than comparing pooled means.

**7. Fix preprocessing and group definitions per dataset, and state every undocumented choice.** South German Credit (not Statlog) with Grömping's corrected coding; response recoded so that "bad" is the positive/rare class; primary attribute age with the 25 cut and the side of 25 stated (recommend ≤25 unprivileged per AIF360, which matches Le Quy's 190/810 split); `famges` used only as a secondary attribute with level 2 reported as mixed-sex rather than relabelled; foreign worker excluded as too small ([UCI 522](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/522/south+german+credit); [AIF360 docs](https://aif360.readthedocs.io/en/latest/modules/generated/aif360.datasets.GermanDataset.html); [Le Quy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00530)). GMSC: drop the 269 sentinel-96/98 rows (or cap and add a flag, but say which), drop age = 0, impute MonthlyIncome and NumberOfDependents with median plus a missing indicator, log-transform utilisation, debt ratio and income, de-duplicate, keep age < 25 as the comparability group and document that no fairness paper specifies these steps ([Lessmann et al. 2015](https://crc.business-school.ed.ac.uk/sites/crc/files/2023-10/Benchmarking-State-of-the-Art-Classification-Algorithms-for-Credit-Scoring-Lessmann-Seow-Baesens-and-Thomas.pdf); [OpenML 45577](https://www.openml.org/api/v1/json/data/45577)). Taiwan: fold EDUCATION 0/5/6 into 4 and MARRIAGE 0 into 3, keep PAY codes −2/0 as distinct categories (documented in the Yeh–Lien replication as "no consumption" and "revolving credit"), use all 30,000 rows and state so, with sex as the primary attribute and marriage, education and age (< 25) as additional candidate attributes for AFCP's selection ([UCI 350](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/350/default+of+credit+card+clients); [NCCU replication](https://ah.lib.nccu.edu.tw/bitstream/140.119/135850/1/133.pdf); [Le Quy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00530)). Do not resample for class imbalance, in line with the fairness benchmarks ([arXiv 2412.20298](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20298v1)). Commit the two UCI files with CC BY attribution and GMSC as a download script with row count and hash.

## Conclusion

The literature's lesson is that fairness-aware CP is a per-cell science dressed as a per-dataset one. Every guarantee (Romano's per-group quantile, Ding's per-class quantile, Vadlamani's per-(group, label) bound, AFCP's selected-group set) is only as good as the smallest cell it conditions on, and the AFCP code quietly multiplies the number of cells by the number of classes. A thesis that leads with a cell-count table and a stated resolvable-gap for each dataset will be more defensible than any amount of additional methods, and it will explain, rather than merely display, why AFCP selects nothing on South German Credit, why GMSC's under-25 group is nearly uncalibratable, and why Taiwan is where the comparison can actually be decided.

The bias-injection extension is also where the thesis can say something new, because no study has yet crossed group-dependent label bias with group-conditional conformal coverage. Einbinder's result that one-sided noise under-covers while symmetric noise only inflates sets, combined with Ceccon's finding that label bias bites hardest when features proxy the attribute, yields a testable prediction: under one-sided flipping of the disadvantaged group's good labels, marginal CP should lose coverage on that group's defaulters first, equalized-coverage methods should recover coverage at the price of set-size and singleton gaps of the kind Cresswell et al. warn about, and AFCP's selection frequency should rise with f only once the (group × label) cell is large enough for its t-test to see it. Designing the experiment so that this prediction can fail is the surest way to make the thesis's results interpretable whichever way they come out.
